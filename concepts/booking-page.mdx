Your public page lives at `tikk.chat/yourname`. It's the only part of Tikk a prospective client ever sees, and you decide what it looks like when they get there.

There are two ways to publish it. Both live at the same address, so switching between them never breaks a link you've already shared.

  <Card title="Classic" icon="calendar-check">
    The booking page itself: your photo, a short introduction and a calendar. Visitors pick a time and book. **Always on** — it's what sits underneath everything else.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Website" icon="layout-template">
    A landing page in front of the booking flow — hero, story, services, testimonials, FAQ. Visitors read first, then click through to book. **Optional**, switch it on when you want it.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Find both under **Website** in the sidebar. That screen is the hub: it shows a drawing of each mode, a switch for the website and a button into each editor.

## Choosing a mode

The classic booking page can't be switched off. It's the thing itself, and it's what visitors land on until you decide otherwise.

The website is something you put **in front** of it. Flip the switch on the Website card and your address starts serving the landing page; flip it back and you're returned to the booking page exactly as it was. Nothing is deleted either way — the page you built is still there when you switch it back on.

<Note>
  Whichever mode is live, your individual service links (`tikk.chat/yourname/discovery-call`) always go straight to the booking flow. The website advertises your services; it never wraps the booking engine.
</Note>

## Your page isn't live until you claim a username

A new account has no username, and until you pick one there's no public address and nobody can book you. Claim yours under **Settings → Profile**. Your page goes live the moment you save.

Choose carefully: the username is in every link you share.

## Booking URLs

Your page lives at your username and every service gets its own URL beneath it:

```text
tikk.chat/{username}
tikk.chat/{username}/{service}
```

So if your username is `joeri` and you have a discovery call:

```text
tikk.chat/joeri
tikk.chat/joeri/discovery-call
```

The last part is the service's booking link, generated from its title. See [Services](/concepts/services) for how to change it.

## Sharing your page

Click **Share** in the Website header — or from inside either editor — for four ways to hand out the link:

| Tab         | What you get                                                                          |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Share**   | The plain link, ready to copy into an email signature, proposal or bio.               |
| **Embed**   | A snippet that puts the booking page on your own website. See [Embed widget](/embed). |
| **Social**  | Pre-written posts for the common social networks.                                     |
| **QR code** | A downloadable QR code, for print, slides or a shop window.                           |

## Where everything is edited now

Your public page is edited **on the page itself**. Open an editor and you're looking at the real page, not a preview of it — click a headline and type.

What isn't on the page is edited where it belongs:

| What                                     | Where                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Prices, durations, location, booking type | **Services** — each service carries its own. See [Services](/concepts/services). |
| The hours you're open, days off, buffers  | **Availability**. See [Set your availability](/guides/availability-setup).   |
| Your name, email, username, timezone      | **Settings → Profile**.                                                     |
| Everything a visitor sees on the page     | The editor for the mode you're publishing.                                  |

## The Tikk badge

Every published page carries a small "Make your website with tikk" badge. It's the same on both modes, so it's set once on the Website hub rather than in either editor.

Removing it is a **Pro feature**.
