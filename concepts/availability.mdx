sidebarTitle: "Availability"

---

Every slot on your booking page has to clear several layers before a visitor ever sees it. Understanding those layers explains why a time you expected to be open sometimes isn't.

## The layers

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your weekly schedule">
    The base. You set the hours you're open for each weekday, say Mon–Fri
    09:00–17:00. Nothing outside those windows is ever bookable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Date overrides">
    Exceptions to that base for specific dates. A holiday, a day off or a
    Saturday you're making an exception for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your connected calendars">
    Every calendar you've connected is checked. A busy event overlapping a slot,
    even by a minute, removes it. This is what prevents double bookings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buffer times">
    Padding around existing bookings. With a 15-minute buffer after, a meeting
    ending at 10:00 pushes the next bookable slot to 10:15.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The service being booked">
    Its duration decides how the open time is sliced up and it can carry its own
    buffers and its own date overrides on top of your account-wide ones.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A slot has to survive all of them. One conflict anywhere and it's gone.

## How the open time is sliced

Tikk takes what's left after those layers and cuts it into slots the length of the service being booked. A 90-minute gap offers three slots for a 30-minute service and none at all for a 2-hour one.

There's also a **minimum of 15 minutes' notice**: slots starting sooner than that don't appear, so nobody books you for something starting in four minutes.

<Info>
  Slots are worked out the moment someone opens your page, never cached ahead of
  time. If you accept a meeting in another calendar right now, the matching slot
  is gone from your booking page immediately.
</Info>

## Timezones

Your weekly schedule and date overrides are stored in **your** timezone. Visitors see every slot converted to their own local time, so neither of you has to do the arithmetic.

Tikk takes your timezone from your browser and records it whenever you save **Settings → Profile**.

<Warning>
  That means a long trip can move it. If you save your profile from a machine
  set to another timezone, your stored hours don't move with it. 09:00 stays
  09:00, now in the new zone. After relocating, open your schedule and check it
  still says what you meant.
</Warning>
